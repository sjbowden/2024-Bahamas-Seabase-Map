# The journal

One of the leaders kept a journal in a notebook through the week. This is that
journal, lightly edited: spelling and punctuation tidied, a few private passages
left out, first names only. Passages set apart like this

> were added in October 2026,

when the notebook was transcribed and checked against the GPS track and the charts.

## Thursday 21 March 2024

After months of prep, and pretty significant prep the last few weeks, today is the day!

I didn't sleep great. I was up too late with last-minute prep. We met at Top Golf at 10 am. Tiffany is one of the drivers. Our bags had been pack-checked the night before at Taylor's.

The drive to the airport was uneventful after meeting up at Top Golf, where we took pictures and loaded everyone into the cars going to the airport.

Tiffany dropped us off at the curb and we grabbed our backpacks out of Taylor's pickup truck, where we had put them after pack check.

We were all in one group reservation. Fortunately, there was not much of a line at the check-in counter. It takes a while to check in 20 people. One of the gate agents noticed we were in boarding group 8 and called up to the gate and requested that we board with group 5, to ensure that we would not need to check our carry-on bags. Security went pretty fast.

After security we went to our gate. We hung out for a bit and then went in search of food. Kyle and I split an Italian Potbelly sandwich. Later, I helped Caleb get one too. We had plenty of time to hang out because our flight didn't leave until 1:54 pm.

Boarding went pretty well because we were allowed to board with group 5. We were in the back of the plane — 3 whole rows plus 2 seats.

We left a little late, at 2:17 pm, with a warning that there were thunderstorms in Dallas. It was only supposed to be about a 3½ hour flight, but about the time we got close to Dallas, the thunderstorms got intense and we were diverted to San Antonio. Unfortunately there were also storms in San Antonio. We circled for a long time, landing at 9:40 pm, over two hours later than we were supposed to land in Dallas. Once on the ground in San Antonio we did not go to a gate, but instead parked in an open area far from the terminal. They let people off who wanted and bused them to a terminal. They did not get their checked bags and would not be allowed back on the plane. We took on fuel and waited out the storm. We eventually left San Antonio at 11:09 pm. We had to wait to get a flight plan approved that would miss the storms. It then took longer than normal to get to Dallas, both because of weather and the many planes trying to get to Dallas. We circled but then arrived at 12:02 am.

## Friday 22 March 2024

The airline emailed food vouchers to Taylor: $12 per person. Dave then requested more (he was actually trying to get flight vouchers), so we had $480 in vouchers with no open restaurants to take them. We ended up getting food from 7-11, which was open, but did not accept vouchers.

I got Caleb a sausage egg muffin, which we heated in the 7-11 microwave. Kyle and I got Chobani drink yogurts and different salami and cheese packages — his with crackers, mine with olives.

About half the group spread out sleeping pads and blankets/bags and napped. I put in earplugs and put on an eye mask. I dozed for about an hour but heard lots of sounds. I gave up when a flight came in right next to where we were sleeping. They had taken off from Dallas, flown for a couple of hours and then returned to Dallas. So the passengers were yelling and the gate agents were making announcements over the PA system. So I gave up. Then David came over with $240 in food vouchers (the other $240 had arrived by email to Taylor, and he was asleep). David was told there was a Panera Bread open 24/7, but it was in a different terminal. So Kyle, a handful of other boys and I rode the train to the other terminal. It took 20–25 minutes because trains were only running in one direction, so we had to take the long way around. The Panera was closed. So, back to our gate (only a few minutes, because it was one terminal away in the direction we wanted).

> Some people napped or outright slept. An airport employee asked about our sleeping pads and bags, because they were thinking about starting a business: a vending machine with supplies for people who had long layovers or missed connections.

Once back at the gate we just talked while some people slept. Kyle dozed, and Caleb had slept while we were riding the train. Finally at 4 am we packed up and got ready to board. Dave tried to get them to let us board early, but they wouldn't this time. Fortunately, when we boarded we didn't have any trouble getting all the bags on and stored in overhead bins. The flight stayed on schedule, but was very bumpy, so we didn't get snacks or drinks. Many people slept, unlike the first flight, when they chatted, goofed off, and watched movies on the inflight system. We landed in Miami a few minutes late. We were a little nervous because we only had a 1.5 hour layover. We walked through the airport to get to our new gate.

At the gate, I asked the gate agent exactly where to go (it was confusing because there were several doors and many gate numbers). The gate agent then asked me how many backpacks there were of the size I was wearing. I replied 20, and he called ahead to warn them and printed 20 luggage tags. We didn't have time to eat before the plane boarded, so we had been going for a while without food. When we boarded the plane it wasn't clear where to drop our bags, so my group (Caleb, Kyle, Brycen, Alexey) got to the plane door and had to go back down the ramp and along the walkway to put them on the cart (which now had baggage handlers; it didn't before). We boarded, got situated and then flew the 31-minute flight.

The plane was an Embraer 175 with seats two and two. On the flight we spent most of the time filling out paperwork for Bahamian Immigration. It was beautiful to fly into Marsh Harbour: clear blue water and lots of marshy islands. We landed, got off the plane and entered the very small terminal. The immigration officials recognized we were a scout troop and were happy to see us. Immigration went pretty well — we only had to show paperwork for one of the youth (i.e. the paperwork showing that the youth could travel with us). The officials were very laid back. Then we claimed our backpacks and the two troop gear bags. Customs went well. I had registered my drone with the Bahamas' equivalent of the FAA. The paperwork said it had to be shown to Customs. I did that, but it seemed to confuse them. Regardless, they verified that the serial number matched the paperwork. On the drone the serial number is nearly microscopic, so it took the Customs agent a few minutes.

We then left into the main part of the airport. Several people used the bathroom while others figured out transportation. They arranged three taxis. It was a 5 to 10 minute drive to the hotel, Abaco Beach Resort. We left our bags at the front desk and went to a restaurant the Immigration/Customs employees recommended. On the way we stopped at a small grocery store. We mainly just scoped it out, but a few people bought sodas. The restaurant, named Colors, is a Bahamian restaurant.

We were in the Bermuda Triangle.

We started with conch fritters. They are a little like hushpuppies with pieces of conch inside. Jeff and I each got grilled shrimp. Kyle got coconut shrimp and Caleb got a conch burger. Dave got conch cooked in aluminum foil. Taylor got a chicken quesadilla. The food was great. After lunch we went back to the hotel and got our rooms. Jeff and a couple of boys stopped at a bigger grocery store to buy plastic water bottles. Two of the boys lost their water bottles either in an airport or in an airplane.

We changed into swimsuits and played on the beach behind the hotel. We swam in the ocean and relaxed on mats anchored a little offshore. It wasn't the greatest weather — cloudy and a bit of wind — but it was warm and we had fun. Besides swimming, some boys played on the sand, laid on the beach, or played chess on a large-scale set. Later, some people napped and others hung out.

The adults decided that we would go back to the grocery store and buy stuff for dinner and breakfast. We then had to wake people up so we could shop before the store closed.

At the grocery store we bought lots of random stuff. The adults got a cheese and meat platter plus fruit and yogurt. A lot of kids got Cup Noodles ramen, quick mac 'n' cheese, and cereal and milk for breakfast. They used the coffee maker to heat water for the ramen and mac 'n' cheese.

After dinner we all just relaxed. Some boys walked on the beach and the adults just talked in our room. We went to bed early.

## Saturday 23 March 2024

We woke up, repacked our backpacks and ate our breakfast. We then walked with our gear to the Conch Inn, where our ship was docked, and we met the organizers of Sea Base Bahamas. Before we met them we ate at Snappa's restaurant. Jeff and I split shrimp and grits. Most of the others got a burger that came with an egg on top. After lunch the Sea Base organizer met us and gave us an orientation. They also oriented another troop. We talked to the troop — they are from Colorado. They wanted to do our trip, but we had beaten them to the reservation (January 2023). Instead their troop split into two, each group on a smallish monohull boat. They arrived a day late due to missing a flight caused by weather delays. They didn't get all their gear — luggage lost by the airlines. The other troop had not followed instructions and they packed their forms in checked luggage. They were lucky the captains let them on without liability waivers.

We had our orientation and then met our captain, Josh. We then boarded the Adonai, a 56-foot catamaran. The mast of the Adonai is 78 feet tall.

Once aboard, the captain showed us where to put our bags. The places were technically bedrooms, but certainly not big enough for all the people assigned to them to fit to sleep.

It was rainy and we hurried out of port to beat a storm blowing in.

One thing we were supposed to do right away was a swim check. But as soon as we dropped anchor we heard thunder, so we didn't do it then. We waited for the storm to pass. We then did a swim and snorkel check. There wasn't anything to see, but we circled the boat, swam between the hulls and swam down to touch the anchor, about 10 feet deep. We then made dinner (burgers), did a devotional, and went to bed. Because there was rain during the night, everyone found a place inside. It was pretty crowded. Jeff and I shared a small room. I slept pretty well.

## Sunday 24 March 2024

In the morning, we got up around 7 am. The cooking crew made eggs and potatoes. Jeff and I were on the cleaning crew. We washed dishes by scrubbing them with a soap-filled scrubber sponge. We sat on the back steps of the boat and used sea water. Then we would rinse, also with sea water. Finally, we would barely spritz them with fresh water from the fresh water tank, and laid them out to dry on the outside covered table.

Then our captain, Josh, gave us a sailing orientation — named parts, how sailing works, sails, etc. He then gave us fishing training.

Afterwards, the deck crew helped Josh (and Thomas, the first mate) raise the sails and we sailed to the outer reef. Once there we got together with buddies and put on fins and snorkel gear. The water wasn't calm, but it wasn't really rough. It was clear and we could see lots of fish. I saw: sergeant major fish, blue tang, a grouper, and a parrotfish (I think a stoplight parrotfish). A few people found cool shells. All the fish were around clusters of coral.

Then we motored to a calmer place to have lunch.

Lunch was pretty simple — deli meat and cheese on sliced bread with small bags of chips.

We then went to a beach. We swam from the boat to the beach. It was almost just a sandbar, but it connects to the main beach. Some of the boys played frisbee and football. There are two swings there, just in very shallow water, so we took pictures of us swinging on the swing. It was sunny, the water was clear and it wasn't very crowded. It was very enjoyable.

> This was Tahiti Beach, on the south end of Elbow Cay. The GPS track has the boat about 260 m off it from 12:51 to 2:29 pm.

The beach was at low tide. At high tide it is covered by water.

We swam back to the boat and sailed/motored to a shipwreck. The wreck is some sort of a barge, only about 10 feet deep. The ship was covered in coral. There were fish everywhere. We snorkeled all around the ship. The water was very clear. The water felt cool, but not cold.

After the shipwreck we sailed to a place where we hoped to catch conch. Not all of us got in the water. Unfortunately no one found big enough conch to keep. There were some cool shells though.

We then motored to a place with calmer water. We had dinner — spaghetti with meatballs. We had another devotional and went to bed.

Downtime on the boat:

- sitting in the outdoor eating area (the cockpit) chatting
- card games in the salon
- laying on the nets or beanbags at the front of the boat
- fishing off the back of the boat

## Monday 25 March 2024

We got up at seven. Jeff and I slept OK. We had cracked open a porthole, which had pros and cons. It had gotten muggy and warm the night before, so the porthole helped. The downside was that we could hear water slapping against the hull, and sometimes a few drops of water would fly through.

Jeff and I were part of the dining crew. Fortunately breakfast was easy — breakfast cereal and milk.

We then sailed for a while until we got to Hope Town. At Hope Town, we anchored the Adonai and took the dinghy in, 10 people at a time. We first visited the lighthouse. It is the oldest operating kerosene-powered lighthouse. We climbed up the narrow spiral stairs; there were beautiful views from the top. The sun was out and it was beautiful. The water was clear. After the lighthouse, Josh ferried us over to Hope Town proper. There, we wandered around. We stopped at Vernon's Grocery and looked around. Vernon told us the famous pies wouldn't be available until 2–3 pm. We stopped at the lighthouse gift shop and several boys bought items. Then we went to a gift shop and more people bought things. Then we went to Captain Jack's for more Bahamian cuisine. We had more conch fritters. Caleb had mahi fish sticks. Kyle and I split a three-fish-taco plate. It was all pretty good. We had WiFi, so we all communicated with home, uploaded/shared photos, and I kept my streak on Duolingo.

> The shop's name is Vernon's Store. It is on Queen's Highway in Hope Town, at plus code G2RR+2FP.

After lunch we visited a pink sand beach, bought more souvenirs and got ice cream. We had arranged to meet the other half of the group. We had crossed paths with them (the 10 that included Dave and Taylor) at the restaurant. We arranged to meet them at 2 pm in front of Vernon's. At 2 pm the grocery opened after their lunch break. We asked about pie and found out they wouldn't be ready for at least an hour, so we gave up on that. We had to wait for the other group about 15 minutes. Then we went to the public dock and Josh picked us up.

Once everyone was back on board, we sailed south. We mostly relaxed, re-sunscreened, etc. We sailed through the area where we had gone to the beach with the swing. We didn't stop, but we went slow because it was a super low tide. The draft of the Adonai is 4.5 feet. At one point the depth meter was reading 4.6 feet.

We sped up afterwards by letting out more sail. We just hung out and talked. I took a bunch of pictures. Those who didn't eat at the restaurant had deli sandwiches.

One funny thing that happened was Kyle asking me questions by saying, "Dad…" The captain said that every time Kyle says "Dad…" he had to do 10 pushups. Within a few minutes Kyle had to do two sets.

We sailed to a beach. There we unloaded two kayaks and two paddleboards. Some people swam to the beach. We used the kayaks to ferry people and dinner supplies to the beach.

I put my drone case in a 2-gallon Ziploc and rode in a kayak to shore. Once on the beach I flew my drone, but it was so bright that I didn't get great shots. We built a fire on the beach from wood we scavenged. We also followed a path to the other side of the island. On that side the waves are much rougher and the shore much rockier. We cooked hot dogs and chili over the fire. Also on the island were several older couples cooking food over their own fire. One of the men came over to talk. His grandson is a Boy Scout, so he filmed a short video of us saying hello.

I cooked two hot dogs, one for Kyle and one for me. After eating, I flew my drone a bit more. I felt like I got better shots. After a bit I landed, and then we packed up and ferried/swam back to the ship. We washed off sand and loaded the kayaks and paddleboards back on the storage racks.

Then we took Joy baths. Basically, lather up with Joy dish liquid and then jump into the water to rinse off.

As we reboarded the ship we saw a plume in the sky. At first we thought it was a jet contrail, but it kept going. We then saw two bright objects separate from it. The main ship had a very distinctive plume. It was confirmed when we saw one of the objects light a flame. We were watching a SpaceX launch! The two bright objects that separated were the boosters, and we saw one of them fire its engines to do a powered landing.

The SpaceX launch was at about 7:30 pm EDT on 25 March 2024.

> The launch was a Falcon 9, not a Falcon Heavy, carrying 23 Starlink satellites. It was scheduled for 7:42 pm EDT from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral. A Falcon 9 has a single booster, which landed on the drone ship A Shortfall of Gravitas in the Atlantic, so only one of the two bright objects was a booster; that was the one we saw fire its engines. The other was probably the upper stage or the fairing.

We also saw an amazing moonrise. It was awesome.

We then held another devotional. We are trying to earn the Sea Base Duty to God award, which requires leading and participating in devotionals as well as praying with the group.

After the devotional we put glowsticks in the water, tied to strings, to try and attract fish. We didn't see any though.

## Tuesday 26 March 2024

We woke up at the time we had all agreed (7:30 am), but most of the scouts were asleep. So we relaxed until the majority woke up. It was pancakes and sausage cooked on the grill. We then went to Little Harbour. I was on deck patrol, so I got to help raise and lower the anchor. I didn't yet get to help with the sails because we motored to Little Harbour. We swam to the beach. Some people spent time harvesting, opening, and eating coconut. The rest hiked to an old and ruined lighthouse. We could see the other side of the island, exposed to the open sea, so it was much rougher. There were some intense waves. We tried to find the path to the Little Harbour settlement, but we didn't find it right away, so we turned around, but then found it on the way back. It wasn't very far. It was a small community wrapped around a crystal clear and aquamarine bay with very white sand. We walked through the settlement and then returned to the beach and then to the boat.

While we were exploring Little Harbour, Josh and Thomas went spearfishing. They found some spiny lobster but were unsuccessful at spearing them. The tip of Thomas's spear got stuck in a hole and he had to cut it off. Thomas did bring back a spiny lobster antenna for us all to see and touch.

We then motored to see one of the captain's friends, who might need help turning over a boat that flipped in a storm. He said he didn't need help, so we continued on to Sandy Cay. All morning there were large swells. The captain recommended everyone take their seasickness meds.

We had lunch — Sloppy Joes.

Once we reached Sandy Cay we anchored next to a reef. Captain Josh told us that this reef is a favorite spot of Jacques Cousteau. He called it an inside-out reef because it is inside the breakwater reefs. It is also inside a protected park. It was a great snorkel experience. We saw lots of sergeant major, stoplight parrotfish, blue tang, blue chromis and others. We also saw a needlefish, a turtle, and a large Caribbean reef shark.

James dropped his Kindle over the side of the boat. Fortunately we were anchored in shallow water and someone dove down to get it. He will wait until he gets home to see if it works.

After snorkeling we motored over to Pelican Cay to play on the beach and explore it. Josh told us there were some ruins on the hill on the cay. We never found the path to the top. We also played on the sharp coral (above water). We were watching the large waves hit the ocean side. They would strike the cay and send water 10 feet in the air, then the water would fall on us like rain. Nolan got hit by a particularly large wave. He was wearing Crocs; they broke and he got knocked down. In the process, he slipped and sliced up his feet. Several people helped him to the beach, and Taylor brought a kayak from the boat to put him on to take him to the boat. We explored the cay a bit more, but our mood was down and we still never found the path to the ruins.

I was lucky to not get cut up, because I was wearing Caleb's flip-flops (he was wearing his Tevas). My Tevas, both of them, fell apart at Little Harbour.

Someone also got a conch.

Taylor was our appointed medic and he patched Nolan up. Then we sailed toward Hope Town. As I was on deck crew, I got to raise and lower the mainsail as well as unfurl and furl the jib. It was a beautiful afternoon and evening to sail. Beautiful, and a wonderful temperature. Harrison caught a mackerel on a line off the back of the boat.

They cleaned the mackerel and the conch so we could eat them later in the week.

We anchored near Hope Town just as the lighthouse lit up. The cooking crew made pork chops. Taylor was in charge of grilling. In fact he grilled all three meals. We had pork chops, applesauce, mashed potatoes, green beans and then packets of mini Oreos for dessert. We finished eating after 8:30 pm. We wound down and had a devotional, then went to bed.

## Wednesday 27 March 2024

We woke up a bit later. Everyone is pretty tired. We had cereal for breakfast. At the end of breakfast we sang to Kyle for his birthday. I let him unwrap the presents Tiffany sent — Tropical Mambas and a shark Hot Wheels car. I also passed out birthday hats.

I was on cleanup patrol, so I emptied trash and helped with the dishes.

Then we started motoring to a reef to snorkel. The weather was beautiful. The waves were rolling and a bit big. It was nice though, and I dozed a little on the deck. The reef, Fowl Cay, had water that was too rough. So we headed to Great Guana Cay. After dinghying to the dock, we bought candy and snacks at various stores. Many of the boys got soda too. We then walked to Nippers. There we took the stairs down the dunes to the beach. It is a beautiful beach with big waves (because it is on the ocean side). The water was so clear and blue.

Many of the boys and adults played in the waves. Others threw a football on the beach. Then we visited Nippers — a restaurant, gift shop, bar, and swimming pools. We didn't eat or swim, but of course the boys had to check out the gift shop. We then returned to the bay/harbour. Some of the group tried to buy fishing supplies and a replacement tip for Thomas's spear. The supplies were great, but the tip didn't fit. Kyle wanted to go to the grocery store again. I was reluctant, but I figured it was his birthday. When we got there I saw the boat's frisbee on the counter. One of the boys had left it there earlier. Kyle said he had decided not to get candy. I told him he should, and I would pay for it because it is his birthday. The cashier overheard and gave him a vanilla ice cream Drumstick.

Then we regrouped and discovered that the boat's football and Logan's water bottle were missing. They went back to Nippers and the beach to find them (they were successful). Then we got sub sandwiches at one of the stores. I split an Italian sandwich with Kyle. Caleb got a turkey, bacon, and provolone sandwich. I got jalapeño cheese puffs and Caleb got honey cheese puffs.

We then took the dinghy back to the boat. Then we motored to a nearby wreck, an old barge. There wasn't much there, either of the barge or of wildlife.

So we started sailing. We sailed through an immense area of crystal-clear blue water. The wind was perfect and the sea floor was sandy white, so it looked amazing. While we were going, Josh did something called "Fishing for Megalodon". He trailed a rope behind the boat. Then people would climb into the water and hang onto the rope while wearing snorkel gear. Josh varied the speed between 2 and 6 knots. Up to three people could be on the rope at once. Lance fell off the rope once and we had to circle to get him. Caleb, Kyle, and I did it together. Josh got us up over 5 knots. It was very cool to see the bottom fly by. Then you have to pull yourself along the rope against the current to get back to the boat. It was a lot of work at 5 knots. None of us fell off. I was impressed with Kyle. We had to talk Dave, Taylor, and Jeff into doing it. Two things happened during their turn. First, someone flushed the head, and it went right past them. Second, Dave's fingers cramped and he fell off the rope. So we had to circle around again to get him.

Another thing that happened was that both Harrison and Jeff had their phones in their pockets. They didn't lose them, but Jeff couldn't get his to charge — an error about water in the port. I think it shorted a cable; another one worked.

We sailed a bit more. Then we switched to motor and went to another barge wreck. This one was shallower and there were tons of fish and wildlife: sea turtle, southern stingray, barracuda, pufferfish, Atlantic spadefish, lionfish, and many more. We then took Joy baths — get wet, lather in Joy dish soap and jump back in the water to rinse off.

> We learned about the Fish Rules app, at https://app.fishrulesapp.com/, from the captain, so we could use it to identify what fish could be caught, and where.

Then we motored to Marsh Harbour. Dinner was chicken breasts, conch, mackerel (that Harrison caught) and yellow jack (that Josh caught and froze last week) with rice and salad. As we were finishing our dinner, Josh called an important meeting at the cockpit. We then sang Happy Birthday to Kyle for the third time and they brought out cake for Kyle and for all of us to share.

After dinner and dessert we cleaned the dishes like normal. Then we gathered to do a rose–bud–thorn activity (like highs/lows). However, with 20 of us we just focused on roses. Josh led the activity and then shared his thoughts about the week. He said we are among the best groups he has captained for. He gave us our Sea Base patches and also added us to the Captain's List (meaning one of the best; he doesn't always give it out). It means we get to order special patches. During the week we worked on the Sea Base Duty to God requirements as well. We also sang Happy Birthday to Kyle for the fourth time.

> Sea Base's name for this is the Captains Club Award: a certificate for a crew that prepares well, works as a team and takes care of its vessel, with a patch that can be bought afterwards.

Then Josh explained the logistics for getting off the boat. Then we got ready for bed. Caleb's air mattress had a leak, so I was glad I brought an instant repair kit. Hopefully it holds all night!

## Thursday 28 March 2024

Caleb's air mattress did not hold all night. The patch looks good, so there may be another leak.

We woke up and packed. Several of us saw a dolphin near the boat. It was too fast to get a picture. We ate Pop-Tarts for breakfast so we wouldn't have any dirty dishes. Josh and Thomas went out in the dinghy to take pictures of the troop and the boat. They also took a selfie of all of us.

Then we put our packs on the nets and James assigned us final cleanup tasks. I cleaned the galley with Jeff and helped out with some miscellaneous tasks. Then I went with Josh and two boys in the dinghy to take all the trash from the week to a dumpster on the shore. I also threw away my Tevas.

Then the first group of ten gathered up our gear and took the dinghy to the Conch Inn marina, where we had started. A few people went to a dive shop to find more spearfishing gear for Thomas. While they were doing that, we were using the WiFi to catch up on things.

Josh called a taxi for us. The taxi driver took half of us to the airport and then returned and got the other half. Check-in took forever. The agents had Jeff collect all the passports, and the rest of us waited in the lobby of the airport rather than in line. The boys used the time to look at and buy stuff from the gift shop. Jeff also requested a wheelchair for Nolan.

In the airport we ran into the same troop that we met on the way out. They got their lost bag when a third troop, who got in a day late, grabbed their bag from the airport and then met up with them at Man-O-War Cay.

Kyle bought a little wooden sailboat as a souvenir and I bought a bracelet (for Tiffany) with a Bahamian quarter on it. The quarter has a sailboat on it.

Security went pretty well. Logan took a picture at security, and so they made him delete the picture and then clear the photo from Recently Deleted. Getting Nolan through security was a little complicated. Caleb was randomly selected for extra screening, so he got patted down and they searched his bags.

Once we got through security we discovered that our flight was delayed by an hour due to a maintenance issue in Charlotte. We heard later that it may have been a security issue and they had to rescreen everyone on the plane.

They let Nolan board early with Dave to help him. I asked the gate agent about our backpacks, since the plane's overhead bins are small. We were able to get them checked through to PDX. This made the flights a lot easier, as we only had to deal with day packs.

The flight to Charlotte was uneventful. The delay cut into our layover time, so we were nervous about immigration and customs. Nolan and Dave were put through a different line because of the wheelchair, which unfortunately was slower than the rest. However, all of us made great time through it. We were a little bummed to have to do security again.

One of the later groups through customs found Logan's backpack on the floor. He hadn't realized that he had to take it through customs and then recheck it.

We all went to our gate and then went in search of food. Kyle got an Auntie Anne's salted pretzel (the last one). I got a cup of salted pretzel nuggets for me and one for Dave.

Our flight to Portland was delayed by about 20 minutes, which was good — it gave us more time to eat.

Dave talked to the gate agents about Nolan, and they let Nolan board early and gave him a Comfort Plus seat.

This flight was the most subdued of all our flights. Everyone was tired. This flight was also the one with the least turbulence. I slept and dozed for much of it.

We arrived in Portland about fifteen minutes late. There was a wheelchair for Nolan. We claimed our backpacks at baggage claim. Only Brycen's backpack was missing. He got it the next day.

Tiffany took our family and Jeff's family home, which was nice because we didn't have to meet up with other parents anywhere. We just dropped Jeff and his boys off at their house.

Overall it was an amazing trip. We had no major weather issues. Nolan's injury kept him from participating for two days, but it wasn't severe enough to require cutting the trip short or evacuating him.

We were definitely blessed to have so much work out and so many amazing experiences.

Random thoughts: I banged/scraped my head lots going in and out of the boat cockpit. It was just a little too short and I would forget to duck.
